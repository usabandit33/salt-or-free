Common questions

Is a salt-free conditioner the same as a water softener?

No. Public treatment pages treat a salt-based softener as ion exchange that removes calcium and magnesium. A salt-free conditioner is sold as a scale reducer that leaves hardness minerals in the water. Ask which one is on the bid.

Will Charlotte city water feel different after a salt-free unit?

City of Charlotte water is often described as slightly hard. A conditioner may change scale. It will not drop hardness grains the way ion exchange does. Ask for a before-and-after hardness test.

Do I need potassium salt if I have a septic system?

Some shops sell potassium chloride instead of sodium chloride for septic or sodium-restricted homes. Potassium bags cost more. Ask which salt the quote assumes and the refill cost. This site does not set a price.

Should I buy the cheapest no-salt pitch first?

A low number can be a magnetic unit, a template conditioner, or a softener with unnamed salt. Name salt vs salt-free and the post-install hardness test before comparing price.

Who pulls any permit?

Ask the shop whether a plumbing permit is required in your Charlotte-metro city and who pulls it. Rules differ by town. This site is not a permit office.
